How this is calculated

Due date = first day of last period + 280 days + (cycle length − 28)
Conception date mode: due date = conception date + 266 days

Naegele's rule adds 280 days, or 40 weeks, to the first day of your last menstrual period. The rule assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. In a longer cycle, ovulation usually comes later, so the calculator adds the extra days. In a shorter cycle, the calculator subtracts them. A 35-day cycle moves the due date 7 days later than a 28-day cycle.

A dating ultrasound in the first trimester measures the length of the embryo. That measurement gives a more accurate estimate than the calendar method. Providers usually change the due date when the ultrasound estimate differs by more than 5 to 7 days. If you have an ultrasound due date, use that date instead of this estimate.

A due date is an estimate. Only about 4 percent of babies arrive on the exact due date. Most babies arrive between 37 and 42 weeks. A first-trimester ultrasound gives the most accurate estimate, usually within 5 to 7 days.

Irregular cycles make the period method less reliable, because ovulation does not fall on a predictable day. Enter your average cycle length as a starting point. Then ask your provider for a dating ultrasound. The ultrasound estimate does not depend on your cycle.

The calculator assumes that ovulation occurs 14 days before the end of your cycle. For a 28-day cycle, that is day 14. For a 35-day cycle, that is day 21. Conception usually occurs within a day of ovulation, so the estimate can differ from the true date by a few days.

Add 266 days to the egg retrieval date or the fertilization date. For a day-5 embryo transfer, add 261 days to the transfer date. For a day-3 transfer, add 263 days. In Conception date mode, enter the retrieval date to get the same result.

This calculator gives an estimate and is not medical advice. Confirm your due date with your healthcare provider. See our Terms of Use.
