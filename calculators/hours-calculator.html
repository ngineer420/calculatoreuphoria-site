    Hours between two times, with an unpaid lunch break

    Enter the time you clocked in, the time you clocked out, and how many minutes of break went unpaid. The calculator subtracts the break and reports the answer two ways: as hours and minutes, which is how you read a clock, and as decimal hours, which is how payroll reads a timesheet. A shift from 9:15 am to 5:40 pm with a 30-minute unpaid lunch is 7 hours 55 minutes, or 7.92 decimal hours.

    The break comes out of the total, not off the end. If you take two shorter breaks, add them together and enter the sum — the arithmetic is identical, and where in the day they fell makes no difference to the total.

    Shifts that cross midnight

    If the end time is earlier on the clock than the start time, the shift is treated as running overnight and a full day is added. A shift from 10:00 pm to 6:30 am comes back as 8 hours 30 minutes rather than a negative number. That is the behaviour you want for night shifts, and it is where most quick mental arithmetic goes wrong.

    One deliberate exception: if the start and end times are identical, the answer is zero, not twenty-four hours. A calculator cannot tell "I did not work" from "I worked a full round-the-clock shift", and zero is the safer of the two to assume. If you really did work twenty-four hours, enter the end time as one minute either side and adjust.

    Minutes to decimal hours

    Payroll systems almost always want decimal hours, because a decimal multiplies cleanly by an hourly rate and 7:55 does not. Converting is one division: minutes ÷ 60. The awkward part is that only a few minute values land on a tidy decimal, which is why the quarter-hour is such a common rounding unit.

    Rounding is a policy decision, not a maths one. Some employers round every punch to the nearest quarter hour, some to the nearest tenth (6 minutes), and some not at all. This calculator does none of it — it gives you the exact figure, and any rounding your employer applies happens on top.

    Add or subtract time from a clock time

    The second mode answers the other half of this question: not "how long between these two times" but "what time will it be after this much time". Give it a start time and a duration and it rolls over midnight correctly in both directions, telling you when the result lands on the following day or the previous one. It is the calculation behind "I start at 9:15 and owe eight hours — when can I leave?" and behind working out a hand-off time across a night shift.

    Work out the gap between clock-in and clock-out first, then subtract the break. From 9:15 am to 5:40 pm is 8 hours 25 minutes on the clock; take off 30 minutes of unpaid lunch and you are paid for 7 hours 55 minutes, or 7.92 decimal hours. Enter the break in the field above and the subtraction happens for you.

    Enter the times exactly as they appear on the clock. When the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator adds a full day automatically, so 10:00 pm to 6:30 am comes out as 8 hours 30 minutes rather than a negative figure.
