How this is calculated

M = P × [r(1+r)n] ÷ [(1+r)n − 1]
P = amount financed, r = APR ÷ 12, n = number of monthly payments
P = vehicle price + sales tax − down payment − trade-in value

The amount financed is the vehicle price plus the sales tax, minus the down payment and the trade-in value. The lender charges interest on this amount, not on the sticker price. A larger down payment or trade-in cuts the amount financed and the total interest. The calculator does not include dealer fees, registration or an extended warranty. Add these costs to the vehicle price if the dealer rolls them into the loan.

A dealer often quotes the monthly payment first. A long term makes the payment small, but the APR sets the real cost of the loan. At 6.5% APR, a 72-month loan costs more interest than a 60-month loan on the same car. Compare two loan offers by total interest, not by the monthly payment.

A bigger down payment cuts the amount financed. This cuts the monthly payment and the total interest. It also lowers the risk that you owe more than the car is worth. Keep enough cash for an emergency fund before you increase the down payment.

No. A longer term gives a smaller monthly payment, but you pay interest for more months. Change the term from 60 to 72 months on this page and compare the total interest. The 72-month loan costs more in total. Choose the shortest term with a payment that fits your budget.

Most states tax the vehicle price after the trade-in value. A $5,000 trade-in on a $35,000 car cuts the taxed amount to $30,000. A few states tax the full price. Clear the checkbox if your state taxes the full price.

The APR depends on your credit score, the loan term and the age of the car. Borrowers with strong credit often see rates near 5% to 7% on a new car. Used cars and lower credit scores get higher rates. Check the APR in a real quote before you sign.
