How this is calculated

Mifflin-St Jeor
Male: BMR = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5
Female: BMR = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age − 161
TDEE = BMR × activity multiplier

BMR is the energy your body uses at complete rest. It powers breathing, circulation, body temperature, and cell repair. TDEE adds the calories you burn through daily movement and exercise. The calculator multiplies your BMR by an activity factor to get your TDEE. Eat close to your TDEE to hold your weight steady.

Mifflin-St Jeor is the default formula. Studies found that it predicts measured BMR more closely than older formulas for most adults. The revised Harris-Benedict formula is older and usually gives a slightly higher number. Use it if you want to compare against an older diet plan or app.

BMR is the number of calories your body needs at rest for one full day. TDEE is BMR plus the calories you burn through daily movement and exercise. TDEE is the number to use when you plan your food intake.

The formulas estimate BMR from age, sex, height, and weight. For most adults the estimate is within about 10 percent of a lab measurement. Body composition, genetics, and hormones can move your true number outside that range. Track your weight for two to three weeks and adjust your intake if it does not move as expected.

Eat about 500 calories below your TDEE to lose about 0.5 kg (1 lb) per week. A deficit of 1,000 calories per day doubles that rate. Do not eat below your BMR for long periods without medical advice. The calculator raises any target below your BMR to your BMR for that reason.

Yes. Muscle burns more energy at rest than fat. The formulas do not measure body composition, so they can underestimate BMR for very muscular people. They can overestimate BMR for people with a high body fat percentage. If you know your body fat percentage, the Katch-McArdle formula gives a closer estimate.
