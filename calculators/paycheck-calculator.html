How this is calculated

Taxable wages = Gross − 401(k) − Other pre-tax − Standard deduction
Federal tax = 2026 progressive brackets on taxable wages
Social Security = 6.2% × min(Gross − Other pre-tax, $184,500)
Medicare = 1.45% × (Gross − Other pre-tax) + 0.9% × amount over $200,000
State tax = (Gross − 401(k) − Other pre-tax) × State rate
Take-home = Gross − Federal − Social Security − Medicare − State − 401(k) − Other pre-tax − Extra

The calculator first finds your annual gross pay. For hourly pay it multiplies the rate by the hours per week and by 52 weeks. It then applies the 2026 federal tax to the full year and divides each amount by the number of pay periods. This is the annualized method. The effective tax rate divides federal, Social Security, Medicare, and state tax by gross pay. Extra withholding is a prepayment and is not part of that rate.

Assumptions

  • Federal tax uses the 2026 brackets and the 2026 standard deduction from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32.
  • The estimate includes no tax credits and no dependents.
  • The calculator uses the annualized method, not the IRS Publication 15-T percentage tables that employers use.
  • FICA is Social Security at 6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base, plus Medicare at 1.45% and an extra 0.9% on wages over $200,000.
  • State tax is one flat rate that you enter. Real state brackets and state deductions can give a different amount.
  • The estimate includes no local or city tax.
  • The estimate includes no SDI, paid family leave, or other state payroll taxes.
  • The 401(k) contribution is pre-tax, not Roth.

Your employer withholds federal tax with the IRS Publication 15-T tables and the entries on your Form W-4. This calculator uses the annualized method with the standard deduction and no credits. Dependents, a second job, a Roth 401(k), local tax, and state payroll taxes all change the real amount. The estimate is close for one job with a simple W-4.

FICA is the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. It funds Social Security and Medicare. In 2026 the employee pays 6.2% for Social Security on wages up to $184,500 and 1.45% for Medicare on all wages. Wages over $200,000 pay an extra 0.9% Medicare tax. Your employer pays a matching 6.2% and 1.45% on top of your pay.

Nine states have no wage income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Enter 0 for these states. Other states use one flat rate or a set of brackets. The simplest method is to take your last pay stub and divide the state tax by the gross pay. Enter that percent here. Your state revenue department also publishes the withholding rates.

A pre-tax 401(k) contribution reduces your federal and state taxable wages. It does not reduce Social Security or Medicare wages. A $5,000 contribution in the 22% bracket cuts federal tax by about $1,100 for the year. A Roth 401(k) contribution comes out after tax and gives no reduction today. This calculator treats the contribution as pre-tax.

Estimates only. Your employer, your W-4 entries, and your state rules set the real amount. See our Terms of Use.
