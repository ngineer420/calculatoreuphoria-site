How this is calculated

Population: σ = √( Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N )
Sample: s = √( Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1) )
μ or x̄ = mean, N or n = count of values

The calculator first finds the mean of all values. It then subtracts the mean from each value and squares the result. The sum of those squares is the sum of squares of deviations. Division by the count gives the variance. The square root of the variance is the standard deviation.

Use the population formula when the data set contains every member of the group. Use the sample formula when the data set is a subset of a larger group. The sample formula divides by n − 1 instead of n. This correction makes the sample estimate closer to the true population value.

The standard deviation tells you how far the values sit from the mean on average. A small standard deviation means that the values sit close together. A large standard deviation means that the values are spread out. The result has the same unit as the input values.

The population formula divides the sum of squared deviations by the count n. The sample formula divides by n minus 1. Use the population formula when you have data for every member of the group. Use the sample formula when your data is a subset of a larger group. The sample result is always a little larger than the population result for the same data.

A high standard deviation means that the values are spread far from the mean. A low standard deviation means that the values sit close to the mean. High or low depends on the scale of the data. Compare the standard deviation with the mean to judge the spread. A standard deviation of 10 is large for test scores near 50 and small for house prices.

The standard deviation is the square root of the variance. The variance is the average of the squared deviations from the mean. The variance uses squared units, so a data set in meters has a variance in square meters. The standard deviation returns the result to the original unit. Both numbers measure the same spread.

The standard error of the mean is the standard deviation divided by the square root of the count. It tells you how much the sample mean can differ from the true population mean. A larger sample gives a smaller standard error. This calculator uses the selected standard deviation, sample or population, to find the standard error.
